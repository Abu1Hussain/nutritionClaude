How These Estimates Work

Resting energy (Mifflin-St Jeor equation)

Resting energy is estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, a widely used formula based on weight, height, age, and sex. It is a population-level estimate, not a measurement of your personal metabolism.

Activity multiplication

Resting energy is multiplied by an activity factor (1.2 to 1.9) to approximate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), which includes movement, exercise, and daily activity.

Weight-change approximation

Weight change is approximated using 7,700 kcal per kilogram of body weight. This is a simplified estimate; actual results vary by individual and are influenced by many factors beyond calorie counting.

Macronutrient percentages

Protein, carbohydrate, and fat targets use a prototype split of 25% / 45% / 30% of total calories. This is one reasonable distribution among many, not a universal prescription.

Sugar and saturated-fat limits

Added-sugar and saturated-fat figures are shown as upper limits (calculated at 10% of calories each), not goals to reach. Total sugar shown for foods and meals includes naturally occurring sugars and is not the same measurement as an added-sugar limit.

Uncertainty of predictions

Any weekly rate of weight change is an estimate. Individual results are not guaranteed and can be affected by hydration, muscle mass, hormones, sleep, and other factors. A 0.75 kg/week target is an aggressive rate and may be unsuitable for some people; review it with a qualified professional.

BMI limitations

BMI is a screening measure based on height and weight only. It does not directly measure body fat and does not account for muscle mass, frame size, or other individual differences.

Meal data limitations

Meal nutrient values come from a historical USDA reference dataset. Where a nutrient is not reported, it is shown as unavailable rather than assumed to be zero.
